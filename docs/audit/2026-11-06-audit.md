# 2026-11-06 — aethersafha 0.18.2 security, hardening and optimization audit (P(-1))

**Scope:** all of `src/` at 0.18.1 (34 files, 19,600 lines), the first P(-1) pass this project has had. Deps and the
Cyrius toolchain are out of scope; how this repo uses them is in scope.

**Threat model:** a local unprivileged client talking setu and owning its shared buffer file; a malformed wallpaper
file; daimon's HTTP answers over loopback; plugin messages; guest output captured through kavach. The compositor is
long-running, so a client must not be able to crash it, hang it, or grow it without limit.

**Result:** 44 distinct findings: **0 critical, 2 high, 12 medium, 30 low.** 38 are fixed and 6 stay open, each with a
reason below. No client can corrupt the compositor's memory: that held across all five lenses and two reviews. What
the audit found instead was availability: a client could slow the desktop without limit, grow its heap, or knock the
whole run off the GPU.

## Method

1. **Baseline.** `cyrius fmt`, `cyrius lint`, `cyrius vet` and `scripts/bench-history.sh` at 0.18.1, before any change.
2. **Find.** Five auditors, one per surface: the client boundary, state machines, rendering and memory, service and
   parser code, and measured optimization. Each worked from a shared brief. A finding had to be CONFIRMED by a probe that
   ran, or TRACED through the exact code path.
3. **Repair.** Each repair's regression test had to **fail on the unrepaired code first**. A finding that would not fail
   was to be reported as not reproduced and left alone; none was. Disjoint repairs ran in parallel on copies of the tree.
4. **Review.** Two fresh reviewers read the combined change with none of the audit's conclusions and tried to break it.
   Their findings carry `LC-` and `PX-` numbers, were repaired the same way, and are counted in the result above.

**Agreement between lenses.** Lenses working separately reached the same defect five times, and the worst one three
times from different directions (A2 below), which is why it is rated above any single report of it.

## Findings

Status: **Fixed**, or **Open** with its roadmap item. `*` marks a fix whose agnos arm has never run; its decision logic
is tested on the host through a pure function, and the kernel's behaviour was read from agnos's source.

## Optimization

Medians over at least 5 interleaved rounds of two prebuilt binaries, at 2560×1440, on an AMD Ryzen 7 5800H. All of it is the
CPU path, so it helps Linux and any frame that falls back from the GPU, and says nothing about iron's 7–15 fps (D1).

| What | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Frame, 2 windows | 10,836 µs | 906 µs |
| Idle frame | 6,841 µs | 410 µs |
| Wallpaper band, 720 rows | 53,436 µs | 351 µs |
| Opaque surface composite | 3,430 µs | 740 µs |
| Translucent composite, sa=128 | 19,506 µs | 13,224 µs |
| Glyph pack, 64 cells | 46.1 µs | 4.9 µs |
| Title draw | 24.3 µs | 4.5 µs |
| 1080p PNG capture | 528.6 ms | 31.4 ms |

**Cost.** x86 asm rows in `render.cyr` and `wallpaper.cyr`, guarded with a tested fallback. +14.7 MB and about 26 ms once for
the scaled wallpaper. A 1-pixel-wide fill is slightly slower, 2.17 → 2.78 µs.

**Pixel output is unchanged.** `tests/pixel_spans.tcyr` compares every routine against a per-pixel reference over clipped, 0
and 1 pixel and negative rects, on the asm and fallback arms. Review added a 60,000-case differential fuzz over inputs that
suite skips, with 0 mismatches on either arm, and disassembled both targets to check the asm's register use.

**Measured and not worth it:** `rep` on 1 px spans was 2.5× slower, hence the 8-pixel threshold. The stdlib's `memcpy` and
`memset` are byte loops. Skipping unchanged windows gains 561 → 24 µs but draws stale, because focus, title and z-order are
not in the damage model, so it waits for D2 (roadmap P3). The Linux present is now the larger cost, measured by proxy at
2,140 µs, and the loop has no pacing (roadmap P1, P2).

## What held

- **Memory safety at the client boundary.** `setu_attach_ok`'s bounds, messages out of order, id reuse, fd reuse and a buffer
  shrinking after validation.
- **All 29 arrays** in the render surface against their largest index, under both the local and module-scope rules.
- **Clipping,** on guard pages: 65,536 fill, outline and enqueue combinations at 0, ±1, the edges, 2^31, 2^32, 2^62 and 2^63,
  and 5,733 hostile `render_window` cases.
- **The wallpaper path,** with 31 structured files and 900 mutants: 0 crashes, 0 hangs.
- **Both JSON parsers,** with depth caps and 400,000 mutated cases: no crash.
- **The state machines,** with 400,000 random steps over 20 seeds: 0 violations of focus, workspace or gesture invariants.
- **The repairs themselves,** in review: the window pool over 120,000 random steps on each of 6 seeds, and the grab over 400,000
  routed events on each of 3 seeds.
- **`cyrius vet`:** 30 deps, 0 untrusted.

## Not covered

- ⚠ **Nothing ran on agnos or iron.** Every `*` above and all of R-01's path is host-tested decision logic plus a reading of the
  kernel at agnos 1.57.17. A burn must prove them (roadmap B1, B2).
- The migration notes for 6.7.10–6.7.18 were not all read at 0.18.1: 1,104 of 1,998 items were swept by machine only.
- Deps and the toolchain, by scope. Findings there were noted, not chased: agnos's `#72` and `#73` have no owner gate (roadmap),
  and kavach closes inherited fds only below 1024 (`SPAWN_MAX_FD`; no live fd sits that high here).
- Library-only growth with no caller: suggestions, active apps, agent permissions, gesture lists and stopped recording
  sessions. Each owes a cap when it is wired (roadmap D10).
